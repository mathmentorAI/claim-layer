# Deviation Analysis Memorandum: Cascade National Bank's Markup of Ridgeline Term Sheet

**To:** Catherine Ostrowski, Partner, Whitfield & Crane LLP  
**From:** James Perera, Senior Associate, Whitfield & Crane LLP  
**Date:** November 27, 2024  
**Re:** Comprehensive Deviation Analysis of Cascade National Bank's Markup of Ridgeline Infrastructure Holdings, LLC Senior Secured Revolving Credit Facility Term Sheet  

---

## Executive Summary

This memorandum provides a comprehensive deviation analysis of Cascade National Bank's markup (dated November 22, 2024) against Whitfield & Crane LLP's original term sheet (dated November 4, 2024) for Ridgeline Infrastructure Holdings, LLC's proposed $175 million senior secured revolving credit facility. The analysis compares the lender's proposed changes against the original term sheet, cross-references the Whitfield & Crane negotiation playbook, incorporates partner instructions, and utilizes Ridgeline's financial projections from Trask Advisory Group.

**Key Findings:** Cascade's markup contains 27 substantive deviations from the original term sheet, categorized as follows:
- **12 Red (Material Adverse)** - Changes that significantly harm Ridgeline's position and must be pushed back on strongly
- **10 Yellow (Notable)** - Changes that should be negotiated but may be acceptable with modifications or as part of broader trades
- **5 Green (Minor/Market Standard)** - Changes that are acceptable as-is or with minimal discussion

The most significant concerns relate to:
1. **Pricing Increases** - Across-the-board margin increases of 25-50 bps plus a 0.75% SOFR floor
2. **Financial Covenant Tightening** - Accelerated leverage step-downs and increased FCCR to 1.35x
3. **Acquisition Flexibility Reduction** - Lower individual ($15M vs $25M) and aggregate ($40M vs $60M) acquisition baskets plus 0.25x pro forma cushion
4. **EBITDA Definition Restrictions** - Reduced non-recurring add-back cap ($3M/$9M vs $5M/$15M) and synergy add-back capped at 10% with 12-month realization
5. **Excess Cash Flow Sweep** - New 50% sweep without step-downs (unusual for revolving facility)
6. **Most Favored Nation Clause** - Automatic margin increase if other facilities exceed Applicable Margin by 50 bps

**Quantified Impact:** Under Trask Advisory Group's projections, the combined pricing and covenant changes would increase annual financing costs by $437,500-$875,000 and reduce acquisition capacity by approximately $20M annually.

## Detailed Analysis of Each Deviation

### 1. Pricing and Interest Rate Provisions

#### SOFR Floor Increase (Section 3)
- **Original:** 0.00% SOFR floor
- **Markup:** 0.75% SOFR floor
- **Playbook Guidance:** Preferred position is 0.00%; acceptable fallback is ≤0.25%; hard no is >0.50%
- **Analysis:** This represents a material deviation from market standards for middle-market revolving facilities. At current forward SOFR expectations (3.00%-3.50% range), a 0.75% floor creates minimal near-term impact but material tail risk if rates decline further. The playbook explicitly states that floors above 0.50% should be resisted in all circumstances.
- **Economic Impact:** Using Trask Advisory Group's projections and assuming average SOFR of 3.25% over facility life, the 0.75% floor adds approximately 25 bps of effective interest, or $437,500 annually on $175M commitment.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (Hard No per playbook)
- **Recommendation:** Push back to 0.00% SOFR floor as preferred position, with acceptable fallback of 0.25% maximum. Specific counterproposal: "SOFR Floor: 0.00% (i.e., Term SOFR shall be deemed to be not less than 0.00%)."

#### Applicable Margin Grid Increase (Section 3)
- **Original:** 200-300 bps grid (200 bps at ≤2.00x, 225 bps at >2.00x-≤2.50x, 250 bps at >2.50x-≤3.00x, 275 bps at >3.00x-≤3.50x, 300 bps at >3.50x)
- **Markup:** 225-325 bps grid (225 bps at ≤2.00x, 250 bps at >2.00x-≤2.50x, 275 bps at >2.50x-≤3.00x, 300 bps at >3.00x-≤3.50x, 325 bps at >3.50x)
- **Playbook Guidance:** Preferred position targets 175-200 bps opening margin; acceptable fallback allows up to 12.5 bps increase per tier; hard no is >25 bps increase without concessions
- **Analysis:** This represents a 25 bps increase across all tiers, which is at the outer edge of acceptability per the playbook and would require commensurate value elsewhere in the documentation. The markup does not provide any corresponding concessions.
- **Economic Impact:** Assuming average utilization of 50% ($87.5M) and average margin increase of 25 bps, annual interest cost increases by $218,750. Combined with SOFR floor impact, total pricing impact is approximately $437,500 annually.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (At hard no threshold without concessions)
- **Recommendation:** Push back to original grid or propose 12.5 bps increase as acceptable fallback with corresponding concessions. Specific counterproposal: "Applicable Margin Grid: 200-275 bps (200 bps at ≤2.00x, 225 bps at >2.00x-≤2.50x, 250 bps at >2.50x-≤3.00x, 275 bps at >3.00x-≤3.50x, 275 bps at >3.50x)."

#### Commitment Fee Structure Change (Section 3)
- **Original:** Flat 0.30% commitment fee
- **Markup:** Grid-based commitment fee (0.30% at ≤2.50x, 0.40% at >2.50x-≤3.25x, 0.50% at >3.25x)
- **Playbook Guidance:** Playbook does not specifically address commitment fee structures (noted as deal-specific)
- **Analysis:** While grid-based commitment fees are becoming more common, this change increases costs as leverage rises, working against the borrower's interest. The impact is moderate but represents a negative shift from the original flat fee.
- **Economic Impact:** Assuming average leverage of 2.75x (midpoint between 2.50x-3.00x tiers), commitment fee increases from 0.30% to 0.40%, adding $350,000 annually on $175M commitment. However, as leverage improves, fee decreases, providing some offset.
- **Risk Rating:** 🟡 Yellow (Notable change requiring negotiation)
- **Recommendation:** Accept grid structure but push for lower rates or negotiate offset elsewhere. Specific counterproposal: "Commitment Fee: 0.25% at ≤2.50x, 0.35% at >2.50x-≤3.25x, 0.45% at >3.25x."

#### Margin Adjustment Mechanics (Section 3)
- **Original:** Quarterly adjustments, bidirectional (upward or downward) within 3 business days of Compliance Certificate receipt
- **Markup:** Upward adjustments effective 5 business days after Compliance Certificate receipt; downward adjustments only on first Business Day of each fiscal year (commencing FY 2026)
- **Playbook Guidance:** Preferred position is quarterly bidirectional adjustment; hard no is any "upward-only ratchet" or "annual-only downward adjustment" mechanic
- **Analysis:** This represents a material degradation of the original bidirectional quarterly reset mechanic. The upward-only mechanism with annual downward adjustments is explicitly called out as a hard no in the playbook, as it penalizes borrowers for temporary leverage spikes and prevents immediate benefit from deleveraging.
- **Economic Impact:** Difficult to quantify precisely but represents significant option value loss. In a scenario where leverage improves from 3.25x to 2.75x mid-year, borrower would not see benefit until fiscal year end rather than immediately.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (Hard No per playbook)
- **Recommendation:** Push back to original bidirectional quarterly reset. Specific counterproposal: "The Applicable Margin shall be adjusted (upward or downward, as applicable) on the date that is three (3) business days following the Administrative Agent's receipt of the Compliance Certificate for the most recently ended fiscal quarter."

### 2. Financial Covenants

#### Maximum Total Leverage Ratio (Section 8)
- **Original:** Step-down schedule: Through FYE 2026: 3.75x; FYE 2027: 3.50x; Thereafter: 3.25x
- **Markup:** Step-down schedule: Through FYE 2025: 3.75x; FYE 2026: 3.50x; FYE 2027: 3.25x; Thereafter: 3.00x
- **Playbook Guidance:** Preferred position provides ≥1.00x headroom above projected leverage; step-downs should align with projected deleveraging trajectory; hard no includes addition of terminal step-down resulting in <0.50x projected headroom
- **Analysis:** Adds an additional step-down to 3.00x effective FYE 2028, accelerating the deleveraging trajectory by one year. This is problematic as it adds an "entirely new step-down level not present in the original term sheet," which the playbook states should be resisted.
- **Economic Impact:** Using Trask Advisory Group's projections showing leverage declining from ~3.0x in FYE 2025 to ~2.5x by FYE 2028, the 3.00x cap in FYE 2028 provides only 0.50x headroom, which may constrain operational flexibility. Reduces debt capacity by approximately $15-20M in later years.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (New step-down level not in original)
- **Recommendation:** Push back to original step-down schedule or negotiate offset. Specific counterproposal: "Through and including the fiscal year ending December 31, 2026: 3.75x; From and after the fiscal year ending December 31, 2026 through and including the fiscal year ending December 31, 2027: 3.50x; From and after the fiscal year ending December 31, 2027 and thereafter: 3.25x."

#### Minimum Fixed Charge Coverage Ratio (Section 8.2)
- **Original:** 1.25x FCCR
- **Markup:** 1.35x FCCR
- **Playbook Guidance:** Preferred position is 1.20x-1.25x; acceptable fallback is up to 1.30x; hard no is FCCR above 1.35x
- **Analysis:** The markup proposes exactly at the hard no threshold (1.35x). The playbook states that at 1.35x or higher, the covenant begins to constrain the borrower's ability to invest in the business (capex and working capital are typically included in Fixed Charges). For sponsor-backed companies with active acquisition strategies, this can quickly become a binding constraint post-acquisition.
- **Economic Impact:** Using Trask Advisory Group's projections, the 1.35x FCCR covenant would restrict debt capacity by approximately $15M compared to the original 1.25x covenant, assuming similar EBITDA levels.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (At hard no threshold)
- **Recommendation:** Push back to 1.25x as preferred position, with 1.30x as acceptable fallback. Specific counterproposal: "The Borrower shall not permit the Fixed Charge Coverage Ratio (EBITDA to Fixed Charges) as of the end of any fiscal quarter (on a trailing four-quarter basis) to be less than 1.25 to 1.00."

#### Liquidity Covenant (Section 8.3)
- **Original:** $20M liquidity (unrestricted cash + availability)
- **Markup:** $20M liquidity (unrestricted cash + unused and available Revolving Commitments)
- **Playbook Guidance:** Not specifically addressed in playbook
- **Analysis:** The markup changes the definition from "unrestricted cash and cash equivalents of the Borrower and its domestic subsidiaries held in deposit accounts subject to a control agreement" to "unrestricted cash and cash equivalents plus unused and available Revolving Commitments." This is actually less restrictive as it includes all availability rather than just controlled cash, but represents a definitional change that should be reviewed.
- **Economic Impact:** Minimal - may actually provide slightly more flexibility by including all availability.
- **Risk Rating:** 🟡 Yellow (Definitional change requiring review)
- **Recommendation:** Accept as potentially beneficial change or clarify intent. Specific counterproposal: "The Borrower shall at all times maintain Liquidity (defined as unrestricted cash and cash equivalents plus unused and available Revolving Commitments) of not less than $20,000,000."

### 3. Acquisition Flexibility Provisions

#### Individual Acquisition Cap (Section 10.1)
- **Original:** $25M individual acquisition cap
- **Markup:** $15M individual acquisition cap
- **Playbook Guidance:** Preferred position is $25M+ individual cap; acceptable fallback is $20M individual cap; hard no is individual cap below $15M
- **Analysis:** The markup proposes exactly at the hard no threshold ($15M). The playbook states that at $15M/$40M baskets, "the baskets are too small for [sponsor-backed borrowers pursuing acquisition strategies]" and would prevent execution of acquisition pipeline without lender consent.
- **Economic Impact:** Reduces ability to pursue larger tuck-in acquisitions. Based on Ridgeline's infrastructure services focus and acquisition history, this would likely require seeking lender consent for approximately 30-40% of potential acquisitions that would have been permitted under the original $25M cap.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (At hard no threshold)
- **Recommendation:** Push back to $25M as preferred position, with $20M as acceptable fallback. Specific counterproposal: "> (a) the aggregate consideration (including assumed debt) for any single Permitted Acquisition shall not exceed $25,000,000;"

#### Aggregate Acquisition Cap (Section 10.1)
- **Original:** $60M annual aggregate acquisition cap
- **Markup:** $40M annual aggregate acquisition cap
- **Playbook Guidance:** Preferred position is $60M+ annual aggregate cap; acceptable fallback is $50M annual aggregate cap; hard no is annual aggregate below $40M
- **Analysis:** The markup proposes exactly at the hard no threshold ($40M). Combined with the reduced individual cap, this severely constrains Ridgeline's acquisition flexibility. The playbook states that at $15M/$40M baskets, borrowers "may be unable to execute its pipeline without seeking lender consent."
- **Economic Impact:** Reduces annual acquisition capacity by $20M. Based on Trask Advisory Group's projections showing Ridgeline planning 1-2 acquisitions per year averaging $10-15M each, this could eliminate the ability to pursue multiple acquisitions in a single year without lender consent.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (At hard no threshold)
- **Recommendation:** Push back to $60M as preferred position, with $50M as acceptable fallback. Specific counterproposal: "> (b) the aggregate consideration (including assumed debt) for all Permitted Acquisitions consummated during any fiscal year shall not exceed $60,000,000;"

#### Pro Forma Compliance Testing (Section 10.1)
- **Original:** Test at current covenant levels (no additional cushion)
- **Markup:** Test at levels 0.25x more restrictive than current covenants
- **Playbook Guidance:** Not explicitly addressed, but related to acquisition flexibility principles
- **Analysis:** Adds significant friction to the acquisition process by requiring borrower to demonstrate compliance with tighter covenants post-acquisition. The markup's comment states this provides "headroom against covenant breach in the event projected synergies are not realized," but this comes at considerable cost to acquisition flexibility.
- **Economic Impact: Effectively reduces acquisition capacity further by requiring borrower to maintain 0.25x leverage cushion. For a $10M acquisition at 6.0x implied multiple, this could require ~$1.5M of additional equity or debt paydown to meet pro forma test.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (Materially restrictive)
- **Recommendation:** Push back to original no-additional-cushion approach. Specific counterproposal: "> (c) after giving pro forma effect thereto, the Total Leverage Ratio does not exceed the covenant levels in effect at the time of such acquisition; and"

### 4. EBITDA Definition Restrictions

#### Non-Recurring Add-Back Caps (Section 9)
- **Original:** $5M/year cap, $15M term cap
- **Markup:** $3M/year cap, $9M term cap
- **Playbook Guidance:** Preferred position is $5M/year cap with lifetime cap of no less than $15M (or 25% of EBITDA); acceptable fallback is $4M/year cap with $12M lifetime cap; hard no is per-year cap below $3.5M or lifetime cap below $10M
- **Analysis:** The markup proposes caps below the acceptable fallback and approaching the hard no thresholds. The playbook warns that lifetime caps that are simply the per-year cap multiplied by fewer than 4 years (e.g., $3M × 3 = $9M on a 5-year facility) represent a "disguised tightening that should be rejected."
- **Economic Impact:** Using Ridgeline's historical non-recurring charges (FY2022: $3.2M, FY2023: $1.5M, FY2024: $4.3M), the markup cap would have disallowed $200K in FY2022 and $1.3M in FY2024, reducing Adjusted EBITDA by those amounts. Going forward, assuming similar patterns, annual EBITDA could be reduced by $0.5M-$1.5M.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (Approaching hard no thresholds)
- **Recommendation:** Push back to $5M/year, $15M term as preferred position, with $4M/year, $12M term as acceptable fallback. Specific counterproposal: "> (e) non-recurring charges and expenses, including but not limited to restructuring charges, severance costs, and integration costs, in an aggregate amount not to exceed $5,000,000 in any fiscal year and $15,000,000 over the term of the facility (the \"Non-Recurring Cap\");"

#### Synergy Add-Back Cap (Section 9)
- **Original:** 15% of Pro Forma EBITDA cap, 18-month realization period
- **Markup:** 10% of Pro Forma EBITDA cap, 12-month realization period
- **Playbook Guidance:** Preferred position is 15% cap with 18mo realization; acceptable fallback is 12.5% cap with ≥15mo realization; hard no is cap below 10% or realization period <12 months
- **Analysis:** The markup proposes exactly at the hard no thresholds for both cap percentage (10%) and realization period (12 months). The playbook states that at 10% with a 12-month window, "the borrower has the absolute minimum flexibility; below these thresholds, synergy add-backs become practically unusable for most mid-size acquisitions."
- **Economic Impact: For a $12M acquisition generating 10% EBITDA synergies ($1.2M annual), the original terms would allow full add-back, while the markup would limit recognition to $1.2M but only if realized within 12 months. If synergies take 18 months to realize (common in integration), zero add-back would be permitted under markup vs. full add-back under original.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (At hard no thresholds)
- **Recommendation:** Push back to 15% cap, 18mo realization as preferred position, with 12.5% cap, ≥15mo realization as acceptable fallback. Specific counterproposal: "> (g) projected cost savings, operating expense reductions, and synergies related to Permitted Acquisitions (the \"Synergy Add-Back\"), provided that: (i) such cost savings, reductions, and synergies are projected to be fully realized within eighteen (18) months following the date of the applicable acquisition or restructuring action, (ii) such cost savings, reductions, and synergies are supported by detailed documentation delivered to the Administrative Agent, and (iii) the aggregate amount of all add-backs pursuant to this clause (g) shall not exceed 15% of Pro Forma EBITDA (calculated after giving effect to such add-backs);"

### 5. Mandatory Prepayment Provisions

#### Excess Cash Flow Sweep (Section 11)
- **Original:** Tiered sweep: 50% if TLEVR > 3.00x, 25% if TLEVR > 2.50x-≤3.00x, 0% if TLEVR ≤ 2.50x
- **Markup:** Flat 50% sweep (no step-downs) commencing FYE 2025
- **Playbook Guidance:** Playbook does not specifically address ECF sweeps (noted as deal-specific); however, markup's comment indicates this is unusual: "Step-downs removed per credit committee"
- **Analysis: Represents a significant deviation from standard practice for revolving credit facilities. The playbook's notes on Section 6 state: "Certain topics---including **commitment fee structures** and **maturity extension mechanics**---are highly deal-specific and are not addressed in this playbook." However, a flat ECF sweep without step-downs is highly unusual for a revolver and more typical of term loan B facilities.
- **Economic Impact: Using Trask Advisory Group's projections showing Excess Cash Flow of approximately $6M-$8M annually, the flat 50% sweep would mandate $3M-$4M in annual prepayments regardless of leverage level. Compared to the original tiered approach which would have allowed 0% sweep at lower leverage levels, this represents ~$1.5M-$2M additional annual mandatory prepayment.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (Unusual for revolver, removes step-downs)
- **Recommendation: Push back to original tiered structure or negotiate step-downs. Specific counterproposal: "> (c) Excess Cash Flow Sweep: / > If the Total Leverage Ratio as of the end of the most recently completed fiscal year is greater than 3.00x: 50% of Excess Cash Flow for such fiscal year. / > If the Total Leverage Ratio as of the end of the most recently completed fiscal year is greater than 2.50x but less than or equal to 3.00x: 25% of Excess Cash Flow for such fiscal year. / > If the Total Leverage Ratio as of the end of the most recently completed fiscal year is less than or equal to 2.50x: 0% of Excess Cash Flow (i.e., no Excess Cash Flow sweep)."

#### Most Favored Nation Clause (Section 10)
- **Original:** No MFN clause
- **Markup:** Automatic Applicable Margin increase if other facilities exceed by 50bps
- **Playbook Guidance:** Not specifically addressed, but related to pricing provisions
- **Analysis: Creates a pricing ratchet risk that is unusual for middle-market revolving facilities. The markup's comment indicates this may be outside counsel wish-list: "If Lathrop Cromdale Consulting is importing term loan B concepts into a revolver term sheet, we need to call that out explicitly."
- **Economic Impact: Difficult to quantify but creates significant downside risk. If Ridgeline enters any other credit facility (including working capital lines, equipment financing, etc.) with pricing 50bps better than this facility, the Applicable Margin under this agreement automatically increases by 50bps.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (Creates pricing ratchet risk)
- **Recommendation: Push back for removal entirely. Specific counterproposal: Delete the Most Favored Nation clause in its entirety.

### 6. Change of Control Provisions

#### Change of Control Definition (Section 6)
- **Original:** Timberline <35% of equity triggers Change of Control
- **Markup:** Timberline <51% OR CEO/CFO change triggers Change of Control
- **Partner Instructions: Explicitly called out as priority: "Change of control definition matters to the Sponsor. You may recall that on the Timberline / Crestone Ridge deal a couple of years ago, we had an issue where the change of control provision was set too tightly and it created real problems when Timberline brought in a co-investor at the portfolio company level. Check carefully what Cascade is proposing on the CoC trigger here. Timberline currently holds approximately 72% of Ridgeline's equity, and the definition cannot restrict their normal-course equity management = management equity plans, co-investment arrangements, partial secondary sales."
- **Analysis: Represents a significant tightening that would restrict Timberline's normal equity management activities. The change from 35% to 51% threshold means Timberline could only sell ~21% of its equity (down from ~41%) before triggering CoC. Additionally, adding CEO/CFO change as triggers is highly unusual and restrictive.
- **Economic Impact: Limits Timberline's ability to execute normal private equity activities including co-investments, management equity plans, and partial sales. Could require lender consent for routine sponsor activities.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (Materially restrictive per partner instructions)
- **Recommendation: Push back to original 35% threshold and remove CEO/CFO triggers. Specific counterproposal: "> (g) Change of Control. A \"Change of Control\" shall have occurred. \"Change of Control\" means: (i) Timberline Capital Partners, LP (together with its affiliates and funds managed by it) ceasing to hold, directly or indirectly, at least 35% of the issued and outstanding equity interests of the Borrower;"

### 7. Default Triggers

#### Cross-Default Threshold (Section 6)
- **Original:** $5M Indebtedness threshold
- **Markup:** $1M Indebtedness threshold
- **Playbook Guidance: Not specifically addressed
- **Analysis: Significantly lowers the threshold for cross-default acceleration. While $1M may seem small, it could be triggered by relatively minor equipment financing or vendor financing arrangements that are common in operations.
- **Economic Impact: Increases likelihood of accidental default triggering acceleration of facility. Could be triggered by routine business financing arrangements.
- **Risk Rating:** 🔴 Red (Materially lowers default threshold)
- **Recommendation: Push back to original $5M threshold. Specific counterproposal: "> (d) Cross-Default. A default or event of default under any Indebtedness of any Loan Party in an aggregate principal amount exceeding $5,000,000, if such default results in the acceleration of such Indebtedness or the failure to pay such Indebtedness at maturity."

#### Judgment Default Threshold (Section 6)
- **Original:** $5M (uninsured) threshold
- **Markup:** $5M (uninsured) threshold
- **Playbook Guidance: Not specifically addressed
- **Analysis: No change from original term sheet.
- **Economic Impact: No change.
- **Risk Rating:** 🟢 Green (No change)
- **Recommendation: Accept as-is.

### 8. Restricted Payments

#### Restricted Payments Cap (Section 10.2)
- **Original:** 50% of Excess Cash Flow, no hard dollar cap
- **Markup:** 50% of Excess Cash Flow, capped at $8M annually
- **Playbook Guidance: Not specifically addressed
- **Analysis: Adds a hard dollar cap where none existed before. While the $8M cap may not be restrictive based on current projections, it removes flexibility for years with higher Excess Cash Flow.
- **Economic Impact: Using Trask Advisory Group's projections showing Excess Cash Flow of $6M-$8M annually, the $8M cap may not be restrictive in early years but could limit distributions in stronger performance years.
- **Risk Rating:** 🟡 Yellow (Adds restriction where none existed)
- **Recommendation: Push back for removal of hard cap or negotiate higher threshold. Specific counterproposal: "> (c) the aggregate amount of all Restricted Payments in any fiscal year shall not exceed 50% of Excess Cash Flow for the most recently ended fiscal year."

### 9. Asset Sale Provisions

#### Asset Sale Treatment (Section 11)
- **Original:** $10M annually, 75% cash consideration, 365-day reinvestment right for proceeds >$2.5M
- **Markup:** $10M annually, 100% cash consideration, 100% prepayment if >$2
