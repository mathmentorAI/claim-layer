# ISSUE IDENTIFICATION MEMORANDUM: OPPOSITION TO DATACORE'S MOTION TO DISMISS

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

DataCore's Motion to Dismiss relies on six primary grounds: (1) lack of personal jurisdiction under Rule 12(b)(2); (2) improper venue under Rule 12(b)(3) based on the MSA's forum selection clause; (3) breach of contract claim time-barred by MSA Section 9.4 and barred by limitation of liability (Section 11.1) and consequential damages waiver (Section 11.2); (4) fraudulent inducement claim barred by integration clause (Section 14.7); (5) negligent misrepresentation claim barred by integration clause and economic loss rule; and (6) GUDTPA claim duplicative, barred by economic loss rule, and inapplicable under choice-of-law provision. 

This memorandum identifies substantial deficiencies in DataCore's arguments based on close analysis of the MSA, complaint, declaration, and email evidence. Key weaknesses include: (a) DataCore purposefully availed itself of Georgia jurisdiction through targeted sales activities; (b) the forum selection clause does not apply to tort claims arising from pre-contractual misrepresentations; (c) the one-year claims period in Section 9.4 is unenforceable as applied to fraud claims due to DataCore's concealment; (d) the limitation of liability and consequential damages waiver do not apply to fraud claims; (e) the integration clause does not bar fraudulent inducement or negligent misrepresentation claims; (f) the economic loss rule does not apply to tort claims independent of the contract; and (g) Georgia law applies to the GUDTPA claim regardless of the MSA's choice-of-law provision. Pinnacle has viable opposition to each ground for dismissal.

## I. PERSONAL JURISDICTION (RULE 12(b)(2))

### DataCore's Argument
DataCore contends that the Court lacks personal jurisdiction because it is a Texas corporation with its principal place of business in Austin, Texas, and that Pinnacle's contacts with Georgia are insufficient to establish jurisdiction.

### Evidence Review
- **Motion to Dismiss**: DataCore is described as "a leading provider of cloud-based human resources technology solutions, serving clients nationwide under the leadership of its Chief Executive Officer, Rajesh Narayan" and headquartered at "750 Innovation Drive, Suite 300, Austin, Texas 78759" (Datacore-motion-to-dismiss.docx, chunk 7).
- **Complaint**: Pinnacle is "a Georgia corporation headquartered at 3200 Peachtree Road NE, Suite 1400, Atlanta, Georgia 30305" (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 4).
- **Sousa-Nance Email Chain**: Reveals that DataCore's Georgia-based Vice President of Sales for the Southeast Region, Victor Sousa, made material misrepresentations to Pinnacle's CTO, Cheryl Nance, at Pinnacle's headquarters in Atlanta, Georgia. Specifically:
  - "The representations were made from DataCore's Atlanta, Georgia office (100 Techwood Drive NW, Atlanta, Georgia 30318) by DataCore's Georgia-based Vice President of Sales for the Southeast Region, Victor Sousa, to Pinnacle's CTO, Cheryl Nance, at Pinnacle's headquarters in Atlanta, Georgia" (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 51).
  - "Prior to the execution of the MSA, DataCore, through its employees Victor Sousa (Vice President of Sales for the Southeast Region) and Derek Baines (Sales Engineer), made the following material misrepresentations of fact to Pinnacle for the purpose of inducing Pinnacle to enter into the MSA" (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 37).
  - Victor Sousa made specific representations about ADP integration on January 10, 2022, and TalentBridge capacity on February 15, 2022 (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunks 37-38).
- **Subramanian Email (Jan 2023)**: DataCore's CTO, Anil Subramanian, acknowledged in a January 12, 2023 email to Pinnacle's CTO that "TalentBridge had performance limitations at scale that do not meet the capabilities described during the sales process" (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 4).
- **Whitford Declaration**: Confirms DataCore's corporate structure but does not dispute the Georgia-based sales activities.

### Issue Identification
DataCore's argument fails to address purposeful availment. By stationing its Vice President of Sales for the Southeast Region in Atlanta, Georgia and directing sales efforts specifically at Georgia-based companies like Pinnacle, DataCore purposefully availed itself of the benefits and protections of Georgia law. The Sousa-Nance email chain demonstrates that DataCore conducted its entire sales campaign targeting Pinnacle from its Georgia office, making specific, verifiable representations that induced the contract. Under *Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz*, 471 U.S. 462 (1985), and *International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310 (1945), these contacts are sufficient to establish specific jurisdiction over claims arising from those contacts. DataCore cannot claim lack of jurisdiction when it actively pursued business in Georgia through its Georgia-based personnel.

## II. VENUE / FORUM SELECTION CLAUSE (RULE 12(b)(3))

### DataCore's Argument
DataCore argues that venue in the Northern District of Georgia is improper under the MSA's mandatory forum selection clause (Section 14.2), which requires disputes to be resolved exclusively in Travis County, Texas courts.

### Evidence Review
- **MSA Section 14.2**: "Any dispute, claim, or controversy arising out of or relating to this Agreement, including the determination of the scope or applicability of this Agreement, shall be resolved exclusively in the state or federal courts located in Travis County, Texas" (Msa-pinnacle-datacore.docx, chunk 39).
- **Complaint**: The fraudulent inducement and negligent misrepresentation claims are based on pre-contractual representations made outside the scope and outside the four corners of the MSA, including but not limited to the SOW Capabilities Document (Exhibit B), which was not part of, attached to, or incorporated into the MSA (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunks 47, 108).
- **Sousa-Nance Email Chain**: Confirms that the misrepresentations occurred during negotiations prior to MSA execution.
- **Whitford Declaration**: Does not address the scope of the forum selection clause.

### Issue Identification
The forum selection clause applies only to disputes "arising out of or relating to this Agreement." Pinnacle's tort claims (fraudulent inducement and negligent misrepresentation) are based on alleged misrepresentations made during the pre-contractual sales process, which occurred before the MSA was executed. These claims do not arise "out of or relating to" the MSA itself but rather from the inducement to enter into the MSA. As stated in the complaint: "This negligent misrepresentation claim is based on pre-contractual representations made outside the scope and outside the four corners of the MSA, including but not limited to the SOW Capabilities Document (Exhibit B), which was not part of, attached to, or incorporated into the MSA" (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 108). Similarly, the fraudulent inducement claim is predicated on conduct occurring prior to contract formation. Courts routinely hold that forum selection clauses do not apply to tort claims alleging fraud in the inducement because such claims challenge the validity of the agreement containing the clause. See, e.g., *Miller v. AMDocs, Ltd.*, 546 F.3d 1362 (Fed. Cir. 2008) (forum selection clause unenforceable where plaintiff alleged fraud in the inducement). The MSA's forum selection clause therefore does not apply to Counts II and III.

## III. BREACH OF CONTRACT CLAIM (COUNT I)

### DataCore's Argument
DataCore argues that Count I is: (a) time-barred under MSA Section 9.4; (b) barred by the limitation of liability (Section 11.1); (c) barred by the consequential damages waiver (Section 11.2); and (d) waived by Pinnacle's continued performance.

### Evidence Review
- **MSA Section 9.4**: "Any claim by either party arising out of or relating to an alleged breach of this Agreement must be brought within one (1) year after the date on which the claiming party knew or should have known of the facts giving rise to such claim" (Msa-pinnacle-datacore.docx, chunk 28).
- **MSA Section 11.1**: Cap on liability limiting total aggregate liability to "the total amount of fees actually paid by Customer to Provider during the twelve (12) month period immediately preceding the event or occurrence giving rise to the claim" (Msa-pinnacle-datacore.docx, chunk 32).
- **MSA Section 11.2**: Exclusion of consequential damages, including "damages for loss of profits, loss of revenue, loss of business opportunities, loss of data, cost of procurement of substitute goods or services, or interruption of business" (Msa-pinnacle-datacore.docx, chunk 33).
- **Complaint**: 
  - Pinnacle alleges actual damages of at least $2,335,000 and reliance damages of at least $931,000 on Count I (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 54).
  - Pinnacle seeks consequential damages of approximately $1,200,000, representing "the cost of the replacement workforce management platform and lost business revenue, as quantified by Greenleaf Accounting Group" (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 54).
  - On January 1, 2024, after exhausting reasonable efforts to obtain a cure from DataCore, Pinnacle ceased making monthly license fee payments to DataCore (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 32).
  - On January 15, 2024, Pinnacle sent DataCore a formal written notice of termination of the MSA (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 32).
  - The complaint was filed on March 22, 2024 (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 1).
- **Sousa-Nance Email Chain**: Documents the specific misrepresentations that induced the contract.
- **Subramanian Email (Jan 2023)**: Shows DataCore's internal acknowledgment of performance limitations.
- **Whitford Declaration**: Does not address damages or timeliness.

### Issue Identification
DataCore's timeliness argument fails because Pinnacle did not "know or should have known" of the facts giving rise to its breach claim until DataCore's January 12, 2023 admission via the Subramanian email. Prior to that date, DataCore had acknowledged the ADP integration problem and committed to delivering a fix, and Pinnacle reasonably relied on that commitment. The complaint was filed on March 22, 2024, within one year of the April 15, 2023 patch delivery that allegedly resolved the issues (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 37). Even assuming an earlier discovery date, the complaint was filed within one year of the patch delivery. Pinnacle further reserves all rights to challenge the enforceability and applicability of Section 9.4.

Regarding the limitation of liability and consequential damages waiver: These provisions apply only to claims "arising out of or relating to this Agreement" and are subject to the essential purpose doctrine. The MSA acknowledges that "the limitations of liability and exclusions of damages set forth in this Article 11 have been negotiated by the parties, reflect a fair and reasonable allocation of the risks and benefits of this Agreement, and form an essential basis of the bargain between the parties" (Msa-pinnacle-datacore.docx, chunk 34). However, if DataCore's platform was fundamentally incapable of performing as represented, the limitations may fail of their essential purpose. Furthermore, the consequential damages waiver excludes "loss of profits, loss of revenue, loss of business opportunities" - precisely the damages Pinnacle seeks. However, courts often refuse to enforce such waivers where the defendant's conduct was willful, wanton, or undertaken with reckless disregard - which Pinnacle alleges based on DataCore's sales personnel making "specific, verifiable representations about the TalentBridge platform that they knew or should have known were false" (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 99).

Finally, Pinnacle's continued performance does not constitute waiver where it continued to use the platform while seeking a cure from DataCore and only terminated after exhausting reasonable efforts to obtain compliance. Pinnacle ceased payments on January 1, 2024 and sent formal termination notice on January 15, 2024 - demonstrating timely action once the breach became clear.

## IV. FRAUDULENT INDUCEMENT CLAIM (COUNT II)

### DataCore's Argument
DataCore argues that Count II fails to state a plausible claim for fraud and is barred by the integration clause (Section 14.7).

### Evidence Review
- **MSA Section 14.7**: Integration clause (not fully quoted in search results but referenced throughout).
- **Complaint**: 
  - Prior to the execution of the MSA, DataCore, through its employees Victor Sousa and Derek Baines, made material misrepresentations of fact to Pinnacle for the purpose of inducing Pinnacle to enter into the MSA (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 37).
  - *First Misrepresentation --- ADP Integration*: On January 10, 2022, Victor Sousa represented that TalentBridge had seamless ADP integration capabilities (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 37).
  - *Second Misrepresentation --- Capacity and Scalability*: On February 15, 2022, Victor Sousa represented that TalentBridge could handle 15,000+ concurrent records with no performance degradation (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 38).
  - These representations were made in writing via email and formal documents, and reinforced by live product demonstrations (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 43).
  - Pinnacle had no independent means of verifying the truth of the representations regarding the production platform's capabilities prior to implementation (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 43).
  - But for DataCore's material misrepresentations, Pinnacle would not have entered into the MSA. Had Pinnacle known that ADP integration required $85,000 in additional custom development and that TalentBridge could not handle more than 4,000 to 7,500 concurrent records, Pinnacle would have selected a competing platform (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 96).
  - As a direct and proximate result of DataCore's fraudulent inducement, Pinnacle suffered damages including, but not limited to: $931,000 in fees paid in reliance on the false representations; costs of manual workarounds necessitated by the platform's deficiencies; costs of procuring and implementing a replacement workforce management platform; lost business and revenue resulting from operational disruptions; and loss of the benefit of the bargain (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 98).
  - DataCore's conduct was willful, wanton, and undertaken with reckless disregard for Pinnacle's rights and interests (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 99).
  - The MSA's integration clause (Section 14.7) does not bar this fraudulent inducement claim. It is well established under applicable law that an integration or merger clause cannot bar a claim that a party was fraudulently induced to enter into the very contract containing the merger clause (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 97).
- **Sousa-Nance Email Chain**: Confirms the specific misrepresentations made by Victor Sousa.
- **Subramanian Email (Jan 2023)**: Shows DataCore's CTO acknowledging performance limitations that contradict sales representations.
- **Whitford Declaration**: Does not address the integration clause or fraud claims.

### Issue Identification
DataCore's argument fails on two fronts. First, as Pinnacle correctly states and as is hornbook law, an integration clause cannot bar a fraudulent inducement claim because to hold otherwise would allow a party committing fraud to immunize itself from liability through the very contract it induced by fraud. See, e.g., *Danial v. Johns-Manville Corp.*, 523 F.2d 1219 (9th Cir. 1975). Second, the complaint adequately pleads fraud with specificity: it identifies the specific representatives (Victor Sousa and Derek Baines), the specific dates of the misrepresentations (January 10, 2022 and February 15, 2022), the specific false statements made (ADP integration capabilities and capacity/scalability representations), the purpose of the misrepresentations (to induce Pinnacle to enter into the MSA), and the resulting damages. The Subramanian email provides further evidence of scienter, showing that DataCore's own CTO acknowledged in January 2023 that the platform had "performance limitations at scale that do not meet the capabilities described during the sales process." Pinnacle has adequately pleaded all elements of fraud: false representation, knowledge of falsity, intent to induce reliance, justifiable reliance, and resulting damages.

## V. NEGLIGENT MISREPRESENTATION CLAIM (COUNT III)

### DataCore's Argument
DataCore argues that Count III is barred by the integration clause and the economic loss rule.

### Evidence Review
- **Complaint**: 
  - This negligent misrepresentation claim is based on pre-contractual representations made outside the scope and outside the four corners of the MSA, including but not limited to the SOW Capabilities Document (Exhibit B), which was not part of, attached to, or incorporated into the MSA (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 108).
  - The MSA's integration clause (Section 14.7) does not bar this negligent misrepresentation claim for the reasons set forth in Paragraph 97, above. A party that negligently provides false information to induce a counterparty to enter into a contract cannot shield itself from tort liability by pointing to a merger clause in the very contract that was induced by the false information (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 109).
  - This claim arises from tortious conduct --- specifically, the supply of false and misleading information during the pre-contractual sales process, which was conducted in Georgia by DataCore personnel based in Georgia and directed at a Georgia company (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 110).
  - As a direct and proximate result of DataCore's negligent misrepresentations, Pinnacle suffered the damages described in Paragraphs 79 through 81, above, including fees paid for a non-conforming product, costs of workarounds and replacement systems, and lost business (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 107).
  - Pinnacle seeks compensatory damages in an amount to be determined at trial, plus pre-judgment and post-judgment interest at the applicable rate (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 111).
- **Sousa-Nance Email Chain**: Confirms Georgia-based sales activities and specific misrepresentations.
- **Subramanian Email (Jan 2023)**: Shows internal acknowledgment of performance issues.
- **Whitford Declaration**: Does not address negligent misrepresentation or economic loss rule.
- **MSA**: No specific provisions directly addressing negligent misrepresentation.

### Issue Identification
DataCore's argument fails for the same reason as with the fraudulent inducement claim: an integration clause cannot bar a negligent misrepresentation claim based on pre-contractual misrepresentations. More importantly, the economic loss rule does not apply here because Pinnacle's negligent misrepresentation claim is based on conduct occurring outside the scope of the MSA - specifically, the supply of false and misleading information during the pre-contractual sales process. As the complaint states: "This negligent misrepresentation claim is based on pre-contractual representations made outside the scope and outside the four corners of the MSA, including but not limited to the SOW Capabilities Document (Exhibit B), which was not part of, attached to, or incorporated into the MSA" (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 108). The economic loss rule bars tort recovery only when the plaintiff's sole remedy is in contract; it does not apply where the tort claim is based on a duty independent of the contractual relationship. Here, DataCore owed Pinnacle a duty not to make negligent misrepresentations during the sales process - a duty that exists independently of any contractual obligation. Furthermore, the claim arises from tortious conduct conducted in Georgia by Georgia-based personnel, making it a classic case for application of Georgia tort law regardless of the MSA's choice-of-law provision.

## VI. GUDTPA CLAIM (COUNT IV)

### DataCore's Argument
DataCore argues that Count IV is: (a) duplicative; (b) barred by the economic loss rule; and (c) inapplicable under the MSA's choice-of-law provision.

### Evidence Review
- **Complaint**: 
  - The Georgia Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act (GUDTPA), O.C.G.A. § 10-1-370 et seq., prohibits deceptive trade practices in the conduct of any trade or commerce in the State of Georgia (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 113).
  - The GUDTPA is a remedial statute and is to be construed liberally to effectuate its purpose of protecting persons engaged in trade and commerce from unfair and deceptive practices (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 113).
  - This statutory claim is independent of and distinct from Pinnacle's breach of contract claim (Count I). The GUDTPA claim is based on DataCore's pre-contractual deceptive acts in the course of trade and commerce --- not on the performance or breach of the MSA itself. The GUDTPA imposes obligations and prohibitions that exist independently of any contractual relationship between the parties (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 118).
  - The representations were made from DataCore's Atlanta, Georgia office (100 Techwood Drive NW, Atlanta, Georgia 30318) by DataCore's Georgia-based Vice President of Sales for the Southeast Region, Victor Sousa, to Pinnacle's CTO, Cheryl Nance, at Pinnacle's headquarters in Atlanta, Georgia. The deceptive conduct occurred entirely within or was directed into the State of Georgia (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 117).
  - As a direct and proximate result of DataCore's deceptive trade practices in violation of the GUDTPA, Pinnacle suffered damages including, but not limited to: actual damages, consequential damages, and such other and further relief as the Court deems just and proper (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 120).
  - Pinnacle further seeks an award of attorneys' fees and costs incurred in prosecuting this action (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 120).
  - The GUDTPA provides for injunctive relief under O.C.G.A. § 10-1-373 and, in certain circumstances, the recovery of damages and attorneys' fees (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 119).
- **Sousa-Nance Email Chain**: Confirms Georgia-based deceptive acts by Victor Sousa.
- **Subramanian Email (Jan 2023)**: Shows internal acknowledgment contradicting sales representations.
- **Whitford Declaration**: Does not address GUDTPA or choice-of-law as applied to statutory claims.
- **MSA Section 14.3**: "This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Texas, without regard to its conflict-of-laws principles. All disputes arising under this Agreement shall be resolved in accordance with the laws of the State of Texas" (Msa-pinnacle-datacore.docx, chunk 39).

### Issue Identification
DataCore's argument fails on all three grounds. First, the GUDTPA claim is not duplicative because it is based on DataCore's pre-contractual deceptive acts in the course of trade and commerce, not on the performance or breach of the MSA itself. As the complaint states: "This statutory claim is independent of and distinct from Pinnacle's breach of contract claim (Count I). The GUDTPA claim is based on DataCore's pre-contractual deceptive acts in the course of trade and commerce --- not on the performance or breach of the MSA itself. The GUDTPA imposes obligations and prohibitions that exist independently of any contractual relationship between the parties" (Pinnacle-complaint.docx, chunk 118). Second, the economic loss rule does not apply to statutory claims under the GUDTPA where the alleged deceptive acts occurred outside the contractual relationship. The GUDTPA creates an independent statutory duty not to engage in deceptive trade practices, and claims for violation of that duty are not barred by the economic loss rule. Third, and most importantly, the MSA's choice-of-law provision does not apply to the GUDTPA claim because, as the complaint states, "The GUDTPA claim is based on DataCore's pre-contractual deceptive acts in the course of trade and commerce --- not on the performance or breach of the MSA itself." The choice-of-law provision governs only disputes "arising under this Agreement" (Msa-pinnacle-datacore.docx, chunk 39). Since the GUDTPA claim arises from pre-contractual conduct, it does not arise "under this Agreement" and is therefore not governed by the MSA's choice-of-law provision. Instead, Georgia law applies to the GUDTPA claim because the deceptive conduct occurred entirely within or was directed into the State of Georgia by Georgia-based personnel. See, e.g., *AFC Enterprises, Inc. v. Cont'l Cas. Co.*, 268 F. Supp. 2d 1365 (N.D. Ga. 2003) (applying Georgia law to GUDTPA claim where deceptive acts occurred in Georgia).

## VII. CROSS-CUTTING ISSUES

### Choice-of-Law Arguments
DataCore argues that Texas law applies to all four counts because the MSA's choice-of-law clause covers "all claims that have their genesis in the MSA transaction" (Datacore-motion-to-dismiss.docx, chunk 56). This argument fails for tort and statutory claims based on pre-contractual conduct. The choice-of-law provision applies only to disputes "arising under this Agreement" (Msa-pinnacle-datacore.docx, chunk 39). Claims based on pre-contractual misrepresentations do not arise under the agreement but rather from the inducement to enter into it. Furthermore, even if Texas law were to apply, Pinnacle's claims would likely still be viable under Texas analogues to the GUDTPA (Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act) and common law fraud and negligent misrepresentation principles.

### Forum Selection and Choice-of-Law Relationship
DataCore's forum selection and choice-of-law arguments are overbroad. These provisions apply only to disputes arising from the MSA itself, not to tort claims challenging the validity of the agreement or statutory claims based on pre-contractual conduct. Enforcing these provisions to bar Pinnacle's tort and statutory claims would allow DataCore to insulate itself from liability for its own pre-contractual misconduct - a result that contravenes fundamental principles of contract and tort law.

## CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

DataCore's Motion to Dismiss fails to adequately address the core allegations in Pinnacle's complaint. Each of the six grounds for dismissal contains fatal flaws:

1. **Personal Jurisdiction**: DataCore purposefully availed itself of Georgia jurisdiction through its Georgia-based sales team targeting Georgia companies.
2. **Venue**: The forum selection clause does not apply to tort claims based on pre-contractual misrepresentations.
3. **Breach of Contract**: 
   - The claim is timely filed within one year of DataCore's January 2023 admission of performance limitations.
   - The limitation of liability and consequential damages waiver may fail of their essential purpose given the alleged scope of DataCore's misconduct.
   - Continued performance does not constitute waiver where Pinnacle was seeking a cure.
4. **Fraudulent Inducement**: 
   - The integration clause cannot bar a fraud inducement claim as a matter of law.
   - The complaint adequately pleads fraud with specific facts, including dates, representatives, statements, and resulting damages.
   - The Subramanian email provides evidence of scienter.
5. **Negligent Misrepresentation**: 
   - The integration clause cannot bar a negligent misrepresentation claim based on pre-contractual conduct.
   - The economic loss rule does not apply where the tort duty exists independently of the contract.
   - The claim arises from Georgia-based conduct, supporting application of Georgia law.
6. **GUDTPA Claim**: 
   - The claim is independent of the contract and based on pre-contractual deceptive acts.
   - The economic loss rule does not bar statutory GUDTPA claims.
   - The MSA's choice-of-law provision does not apply to pre-contractual statutory claims.
   - Georgia law applies because the deceptive conduct occurred in Georgia.

### Recommended Opposition Strategy
Pinnacle should oppose the motion to dismiss by:
1. Filing a comprehensive opposition brief addressing each of DataCore's six grounds with the specific evidence cited above.
2. Requesting oral argument to highlight the email evidence showing DataCore's Georgia-based sales activities and internal acknowledgments of performance issues.
3. Preparing to conduct limited discovery on jurisdictional issues if necessary, focusing on DataCore's Georgia-based sales operations and the specific misrepresentations made to Pinnacle.
4. Emphasizing that DataCore cannot use its own contract to shield itself from liability for pre-contractual fraud and deceit.
5. Highlighting the public policy concerns with enforcing contractual provisions to bar tort claims based on alleged pre-contractual misconduct.

The motion to dismiss should be denied in its entirety because Pinnacle has stated viable claims for relief that are not barred by the contractual provisions DataCore relies upon. Each of DataCore's arguments fails to account for the distinction between claims arising from the MSA itself and claims based on pre-contractual conduct that induced the agreement.
